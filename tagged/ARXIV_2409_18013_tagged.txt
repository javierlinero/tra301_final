<TASK> Summarize this abstract for high-school audience while highlight importance of contributions </TASK> <ABSTRACT> <BACKGROUND>Data-driven simulation of physical systems has recently kindled significant attention, where many neural models have been developed.</BACKGROUND> <BACKGROUND>In particular, mesh-based graph neural networks (GNNs) have demonstrated significant potential in predicting spatiotemporal dynamics across arbitrary geometric domains.</BACKGROUND> <CONTRIBUTION>However, the existing node-edge message passing mechanism in GNNs limits the model's representation learning ability.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>In this paper, we proposed a cell-embedded GNN model (aka CeGNN) to learn spatiotemporal dynamics with lifted performance.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>Specifically, we introduce a learnable cell attribution to the node-edge message passing process, which better captures the spatial dependency of regional features.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>Such a strategy essentially upgrades the local aggregation scheme from the first order (e.g., from edge to node) to a higher order (e.g., from volume to edge and then to node), which takes advantage of volumetric information in message passing.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>Meanwhile, a novel feature-enhanced block is designed to further improve the performance of CeGNN and relieve the over-smoothness problem, via treating the latent features as basis functions.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>The extensive experiments on various PDE systems and one real-world dataset demonstrate that CeGNN achieves superior performance compared with other baseline models, particularly reducing the prediction error with up to 1 orders of magnitude on several PDE systems.</CONTRIBUTION> </ABSTRACT>
