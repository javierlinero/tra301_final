<TASK> Summarize this abstract for high-school audience while highlight importance of contributions </TASK> <ABSTRACT> <BACKGROUND>The deterministic (timing) behavior of real-time systems (RTS) can be used by adversaries - say, to launch side channel attacks or even destabilize the system by denying access to critical resources.</BACKGROUND> <BACKGROUND>We propose a protocol (named REORDER) to obfuscate this predictable timing behavior of RTS, especially ones designed using dynamic-priority scheduling algorithms (e.g., EDF).</BACKGROUND> <CONTRIBUTION>We also present a metric (named "schedule entropy") that measures the levels of obfuscation introduced into a given real-time system.</CONTRIBUTION> <CONTRIBUTION>The REORDER protocol was integrated into the standard Linux real-time scheduler and evaluated on a realistic embedded platform (Raspberry Pi) running the MiBench automotive benchmark workloads.</CONTRIBUTION> <BACKGROUND>We also demonstrate how designers of RTS can increase the security of their systems and also quantitatively measure the impact (both in terms of security and performance) of using this protocol.</BACKGROUND> </ABSTRACT>
